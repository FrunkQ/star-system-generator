# Surface temperature, albedo ageing and the lock flag — session notes

Written 2026-08-21 by the "surface temperature and the terms that feed it" session on retirement, at
v2.1.877-beta. **Not a design doc and not a backlog.** It is what cost this session real time and is
not visible from the code: the invariants that had to be discovered, the diagnoses that were wrong in
the row before they were right, and the one decision left for the owner.

Everything durable is already in `engine-map.md` — **PHY-19** (an equilibrium temperature is a power
balance and is never a mean), **PHY-20** (a surface property that varies by an order of magnitude is
a process, not a constant) and **DATA-R18** (a band is a range to draw from; a designation is what a
star IS). Read those first. This file says what a successor should NOT re-derive.

---

## 1. There are TWO temperatures on every body, and the difference is invisible until it bites

`body.temperatureK` is the RADIATING temperature — the one at which the body gives off what it takes
in. `body.temperatureProfile.meanK` is the MEAN SURFACE temperature — the average of its day and
night sides. They are equal on anything well-mixed and diverge with the swing: **Venus 753.6 against
754 and Earth 287.4 against 287, but Luna 270 against 214 and Mercury 440 against 310.**

Seven places ask "how hot is this world" and they must all go through **`meanSurfaceTempK(body)`**
(`physics/surfaceTemperature.ts`). A `??` chain spelled seven times is a rule nobody can find; that
is how the display layer got swept and the physics layer did not (B71).

**The one reader that CANNOT use the mean, and why it is not an oversight:** the liquid-water gate at
`SystemProcessor.ts:692` is an INPUT to the profile that produces the mean — an ocean is what
redistributes the heat — so asking for the mean there is circular, and reading last pass's would
break PHY-1. It is also the case where the two agree, because a world with a standing ocean has the
damping that makes them agree. The comment says so in place; do not "fix" it.

## 2. Which temperature the CLASSIFIER reads — ASKED, ANSWERED, AND DONE

**SETTLED.** Owner, 2026-08-21: *"this is a proper physical fix for a 'not properly defined temp' — locking onto
the wrong value. If that corrects things reliably just do it."* Done at v2.1.886-beta; what follows is
why, and what it moved.

**What it was.** `SystemProcessor.ts:1034` fed the classifier `SurfaceTemp_K: body.temperatureK`
— the RADIATING figure. **Seventeen fingerprints key on it**, including every band a reader would
call a surface claim: `planet/ocean` 255-370, `planet/ice` 0-190, `planet/desert` 260-360,
`planet/earth-analogue` 255-300, `planet/eyeball` 255-320, `planet/lava` 1200-6000.

**Why it matters, honestly: little today, dangerously later.** Mercury and Luna fail those bands for
other reasons, so nothing visible moves. The failure mode that bites is a body NEAR a threshold — an
airless slow rotator radiating at 280 K with a surface mean of 220 K matches `planet/ocean` and
should not. That world is generatable today.

**WHAT WAS DONE — the FEATURE moved, the field did not.**

- `SurfaceTemp_K` now reads `meanSurfaceTempK(body)`. Every fingerprint keyed on it asks a surface
  question, and the name already said surface — leaving it was PHY-2 with seventeen consumers.
- **Leave `body.temperatureK` exactly as it is.** It is the radiating figure; the thermal fixed point
  and every emitter depend on it, and turning it into an arithmetic mean would break energy
  conservation downstream. This is not a change of model.
- **MEASURED, and the churn was smaller than the warning below expected: six bodies moved and NO
  CLASS AND NO WINNER CHANGED ON ANY OF THEM.** Only the recorded band VALUES moved — what the
  Newton trace shows as the feature's reading — plus one honest runner-up: Mercury gains
  `planet/desert` as a CANDIDATE at 1.18, because a mean of 37 C really is desert-range, and it
  still loses to terrestrial at 1.20. Pinned in `physics-baseline.test.ts` on Ganymede, whose two
  figures differ (118 K radiating, 113 K mean).
- **The band EDGES were calibrated against the radiating figure and were NOT adjusted.** Nothing in
  Sol needed it, but Sol is 40 bodies with mostly small swings. If a generated world lands wrong,
  suspect the band before the model — the caution stands even though the switch itself was clean.

## 3. The fixture diff is the review tool, and there is a right way to read it

`tests/output/solar-system-derived.json`, regenerated by `physics-baseline.test.ts` on every
`vitest run`. For a physics change it is the primary evidence, not a side effect. Three traps:

1. **Diff tags as SETS, never by index.** Tag arrays reorder freely, so an index-wise diff reported
   34 phantom `tags[].key` changes where ONE tag had actually moved. Build a set of `key=value` per
   body and take the difference both ways.
2. **Run the field-frequency pass FIRST** — walk both trees and count which *field paths* changed and
   on how many bodies. That is what tells you a change was contained (B68: five bodies, every changed
   path downstream of `albedoBreakdown`) before you read any body at all.
3. **The fixture does NOT go through `fixUpImportedSystem`.** The baseline test has its own
   `stripDerivedData`, so anything the LOAD PATH does — the legacy star-class cleaner, for one — is
   invisible in it. `idempotence.test.ts` is what exercises the real load path.

A no-op run must produce a byte-identical fixture apart from line endings. Capture the baseline
before you start (`git show HEAD:tests/output/solar-system-derived.json > ...`) or you are diffing
against your own change.

## 4. Calibration: check against every anchor, and expect to be wrong twice first

Both of B68's wrong turns looked right in reasoning and were caught only by re-measuring:

- **Raising a shared constant broke two other bodies.** `frost: 0.62` is calibrated on Io's sulphur
  dioxide over dark volcanics; raising it for icy shells took Io and Pluto with it. A frost
  condensing out of the air NOW and a shell that has lain there since the last resurfacing are two
  states of one material and want two numbers.
- **A deposit was darkening ground brighter than itself** — Enceladus's own plume-fall was dimming
  Enceladus. The falling material and the surface were the same substance.

Record residuals rather than fitting them. Ganymede is 0.13 against a measured 0.35 and the entry
says why (its surface is a MIXTURE of two ages and the engine carries one); Altair and 61 Cygni A sit
one subclass out in `starDesignation.spec.ts` with the reason beside them. Two stars are not a
calibration set, and moving an anchor to catch one moves every body that shares it.

## 5. The solar day is not the rotation period, and it keys on the SPIN not the lock flag

`surfaceTemperature.solarDayHours` — the time between one noon and the next, which is what a surface
actually cools over. Venus turns in 243 days and its sun rises every 117; Mercury's 3:2 resonance
gives it a 176-day solar day out of an 88-day year.

**The test is whether the spin equals the orbital period, NOT `tidallyLocked`.** Synchronous means a
face is kept; a captured resonance is not, and reading the flag would have handed Mercury its 88-day
YEAR as a day. Three cases, all needed: synchronous and star-locked gives infinite (permanent faces);
synchronous and planet-locked gives the orbital period (a moon's month IS its solar day); otherwise
the synodic of spin and orbit. Plus one guard — believe `starTidallyLocked` when the body states
neither period, because since B69 that flag is only set for a body resolved as synchronous.

## 6. Despinning has TWO end states, and the older proxies are what bite

B69: `starTidallyLocked` followed the DESPIN verdict rather than the resolved SPIN, so Mercury was
published as having a permanent substellar face — and its own `classification.base` read
`planet/hot-eyeball`, score 1.56, beating terrestrial at 1.2. The baseline test asserted BOTH sides
of the contradiction two lines apart, as though it were the contract.

**The tag chase found the real cost in two RENDERERS, not in the tags** — both asking the despin
boolean where they meant synchrony: `SystemVisualizer` drew a sharp terminator for any despun body,
and `planetAppearance` skewed craters to a leading face and fell back to "locked and not a moon" for
the eyeball, every clause of which is true of Mercury. **Grep for the CONCEPT, not the field name.**
That is the standing rule's "any older proxy for it" clause, and it is where the time goes.

## 7. Albedo sits INSIDE the thermal fixed point — what that permits and forbids

`deriveAlbedo` is one evaluation inside `solveThermalState`. Anything added there moves equilibrium
temperature and everything downstream of it.

**The rule that kept B68 safe: the ice-lag term keys on SURFACE AGE, which is already an input to the
loop, and NOT on temperature.** That makes it a second reader of an existing edge rather than a new
feedback path — which matters because bright condensate is self-reinforcing (colder, so more frost,
so brighter, so colder) and B5's bistability trap is exactly that. A new albedo term keyed on
temperature would open it again.

Per-terrain or per-area albedo is still safe by the same argument: it yields ONE number to the solve.

## 8. Surface age has five values, and that caps every surface law

`geoActivity.deriveSurfaceAgeGyr` returns the REGIME'S CONSTANT for an active world (`RESURFACE_GYR`:
tidal-volcanic 0.002, cryovolcanic 0.05, plate-tectonics 0.2 and so on) and a vigor inversion for a
dead one that saturates at the system age. Measured: **five distinct surface ages across all 40
bundled Sol bodies.** Any law keyed on it can produce five answers.

That is why the ice-lag half-age is short (the three young icy moons all sit at exactly 0.05) and why
2 Gyr and 4.5 Gyr are indistinguishable to it — i.e. why Ganymede cannot be reached without fixing
the age first. Full working in `docs/dev/surface-areas-design.md`, section 1.3 and phase 2.

## 9. The duplicate I shipped, and the search that would have caught it

v2.1.687 added a pack-anchored subclass derivation; `starClassExplain.spectralSubclass` had done the
same job since v2.1.645 and was **listed in `type-vocabulary-prev4.md`'s own SHIPPED header**. They
disagreed — the older ladder put the Sun at G2.6, so the wizard printed G3V.

**I grepped for my own function name instead of for the concept.** Before adding any derivation in a
mature area, read that area's design-doc status header; it costs a minute and it lists what exists.
The older implementation also carried something mine lacked, which survived the merge: it REFUSES to
state a subclass for a giant, because the ladder is main-sequence and Arcturus derives four
subclasses out on it. **When two implementations disagree, take the better numbers AND the better
refusals.**

## 10. Process, because it cost more than any single fault

- **`npm run build` in a fresh worktree fails on a missing `vite` until `npm ci` has run** — and
  grepping its output for the success marker reads that failure as silence. Assert on the marker's
  PRESENCE (`grep -c`), never on the absence of an error.
- **Beta moved four times during one push.** Resolve a changelog conflict by keeping BOTH sides and
  renumbering YOURS; take upstream's version and bump from it. A mechanical resolver mangled the
  headings twice, and hand-writing the top of the file was faster than fixing what it did.
- **`git worktree remove` fails with "Permission denied" on Windows** while a shell still has the
  directory as its cwd. `git worktree prune` plus PowerShell `Remove-Item -Recurse -Force` finishes
  it.
- **`tests/fixtures/solar-system-input.json` and `src/lib/generated/exampleSystems.ts` churn on line
  endings alone.** Check `git diff --numstat` (empty means no content change) before deciding they
  are yours to commit.
